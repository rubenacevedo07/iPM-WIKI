# Chokepoint Risk Update — April 2026
**Source URLs:**
- https://www.foxnews.com/politics/iran-backed-houthis-threaten-key-red-sea-shipping-lane-tensions-spread
- EIA / shipping advisories (multiple)

## Hormuz
- **Threat level: 7/10**
- Current situation: region tense due to broader Middle East conflicts; elevated US and allied military presence for deterrence but no formal blockade. Primary risk: incident between IRGC forces and Western vessels causing temporary disruption.
- Key actor: Iran (IRGC)

## Taiwan Strait
- **Threat level: 6/10**
- Current situation: regular Chinese navy and air force patrols around Taiwan; frequent military exercises and flights/manoeuvres near median line. No declared blockade but probability of grey-zone incidents (inspections, warnings to vessels, pressure on shipping companies) remains elevated.

## Bab el-Mandeb / Red Sea
- **Threat level: 8/10**
- Current situation: Iran-backed Houthi forces have carried out attacks and threats against Red Sea traffic, creating risk of "second chokepoint" alongside Hormuz. Shipping companies rerouted via Cape of Good Hope; insurers raised premiums significantly.
- Key actor: Houthis (Yemen)

## Suez
- **Threat level: 5/10**
- Current situation: canal itself remains operational but military activity and Red Sea attacks raise risk on approach/exit routes. Risk is more indirect disruption via Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb than direct canal closure.

## Malacca
- **Threat level: 5/10**
- Current situation: no active crisis; critical node for energy and trade flows Asia–rest of world. Risk more linked to future China-regional powers/US escalation than current incidents.

## Market Impact
- Shipping rates: Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb risks pushed freight costs higher on Asia–Europe and Asia–Mediterranean routes when companies divert via Cape of Good Hope
- Oil premium: chokepoint risk premium in crude prices especially linked to Hormuz and Red Sea; any credible escalation could widen differential vs no-tension scenarios
- Rerouting costs: diversions increase transit times, fuel consumption, insurance premiums; ripples through European and Asian importer costs, adding supply chain pressure
