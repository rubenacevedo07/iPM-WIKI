01Who Is Bill Gates

William Henry Gates III was born October 28, 1955 in Seattle, Washington, into an upper-middle-class family — his father was a prominent lawyer, his mother sat on corporate boards. He enrolled at Harvard in 1973, dropped out in 1975, and co-founded Microsoft with childhood friend Paul Allen. By age 31 he was a billionaire. By 1995 he was the richest person on Earth — a title he held on and off for two decades.

Gates is simultaneously four things that rarely coexist: a software architect who built the operating system layer of the modern PC era; a ruthless monopolist who was sued by the US Department of Justice in 1998 for anticompetitive practices; the world's most powerful philanthropist, whose foundation has spent $100 billion on global health and development; and, as of 2026, a deeply controversial figure whose ties to Jeffrey Epstein, influence over the WHO, land acquisitions, and vaccine advocacy have made him the single most conspiracized private citizen in the world.

He divorced Melinda French Gates in 2021 after 27 years of marriage — paying an $8 billion settlement. He called it "the mistake I most regret." The divorce is now widely attributed to Melinda's discomfort with his relationship with Epstein. As of 2026, Melinda French Gates has stated publicly that Bill "has to answer" for what the Epstein files reveal.

02Microsoft — How He Built It

Gates and Paul Allen founded Microsoft in Albuquerque, New Mexico in 1975 — initially writing BASIC interpreters for early microcomputers. The company's founding insight was not hardware but software as the permanent layer: whoever owned the operating system would own the platform. IBM validated this in 1980 by licensing Microsoft's MS-DOS for its PC — handing Gates control of the industry without him having to manufacture a single chip.

The real power move: Gates didn't invent MS-DOS — he bought it from Seattle Computer Products for $50,000 and licensed it to IBM while retaining the right to sell it to others. This single decision, made at 24, generated hundreds of billions of dollars and established the template for platform monopoly that Silicon Valley still follows.

  • 1975Microsoft founded. Gates + Allen in Albuquerque. First product: Altair BASIC interpreter.
  • 1980IBM deal. Gates licenses MS-DOS, keeps the rights. IBM PC ships 1981. Microsoft owns the OS layer of every IBM clone — permanently.
  • 1986Microsoft IPO at $21/share. Gates becomes a billionaire at 31. Over 12,000 Microsoft millionaires created by stock options.
  • 1990Windows 3.0 launches. First commercially successful version. Office (Word + Excel) bundle becomes the corporate standard. The moat deepens.
  • 1995Gates writes "The Internet Tidal Wave" memo — pivots entire company to the web. Internet Explorer ships free, destroying Netscape — the act that triggers the DoJ antitrust case.
  • 1998US DoJ sues Microsoft for anticompetitive practices. Gates's deposition becomes infamous — evasive, combative, often answering "I don't recall" to basic questions. Judge finds Microsoft guilty in 2000. Company ordered to break up (later reversed on appeal).
  • 2000Gates steps down as CEO, hands reins to Steve Ballmer. Remains Chairman and Chief Software Architect. Begins shifting focus to the Foundation.
  • 2008Gates leaves day-to-day Microsoft role entirely. Ballmer era: missed mobile (iPhone), missed search (Google), missed cloud. Microsoft stagnates.

Azure — Microsoft's cloud platform — is now the #2 cloud globally (behind AWS), with ~$110B in annual revenue and growing. The AI pivot through OpenAI means Microsoft is now the infrastructure layer of the AI era — exactly as it was the infrastructure layer of the PC era.

04Medicine — Vaccines, WHO & Global Health

Gates's pivot to global health began in 1999, inspired by a New York Times article about rotavirus killing 500,000 children a year — entirely preventably. He and Melinda committed the Foundation to health equity. What started as a personal cause became the world's most powerful private health institution.

The Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation (BMGF) is the second-largest funder of the WHO after the United States government, contributing $5.5 billion in 640 grants between 2000 and 2024. More than 50% of those grants were directed at vaccine-related projects. This gives BMGF extraordinary — and critics say inappropriate — influence over WHO's health priorities.

Gates's 2015 TED Talk — "The Next Outbreak? We're Not Ready" — is now legendary. 05Food — Farmland, Agriculture & Synthetic Meat

Bill Gates is the largest private farmland owner in the United States with approximately 275,000 acres across 18 states. His biggest holdings are in Louisiana (~70,000 acres), Arkansas, Arizona, Nebraska, California, and Washington. The land is managed by Cascade Investment and primarily leased to commercial farming operations.

Through the Gates Foundation, he has simultaneously spent billions on African agriculture — funding hybrid seed programs, fertilizer access, and precision farming technology across Sub-Saharan Africa. Critics, including food sovereignty advocates at Navdanya International, argue that the Foundation's "Green Revolution 2.0" approach pushes industrial, input-dependent agriculture on smallholder farmers in a way that benefits agribusiness companies (many of which Cascade holds equity in) more than farmers.

Gates has publicly advocated for wealthy nations to shift to 100% synthetic beef, arguing that lab-grown meat and plant-based alternatives are necessary to reduce agricultural emissions. He has backed Impossible Foods and Beyond Meat financially, and his Foundation has funded synthetic biology research at the intersection of food and pharma. The structural question critics raise: the largest private farmland owner in America advocates replacing conventional beef — which competes with his investments in alternative proteins.

Gates's food thesis, stated plainly: the current food system is incompatible with climate targets. He believes precision fermentation and cellular agriculture will be cost-competitive with conventional protein by 2030–2035, and that governments should use carbon pricing and regulatory frameworks to accelerate the transition. His investment portfolio is positioned accordingly.

12Myths vs Facts — The Conspiracy Map

Bill Gates is the most conspiracized living private citizen — more than any politician. An academic study published in Nature found Gates-related theories were the #1 COVID conspiracy theme on YouTube in 2020–2021. Here is a structured fact-check of the major claims:

MYTH
"Gates put microchips in COVID vaccines to track people."
FACT
Entirely false. mRNA vaccines contain no tracking technology. The theory originated from Gates's 2019 support for digital health certificates (paper vaccination records, not implants) and was amplified via Facebook/YouTube. No credible evidence. Debunked by Mayo Clinic, WHO, EU External Action Service, Reuters.
MYTH
"Gates wants to depopulate the Earth using vaccines."
FACT
Misquote of a 2010 TED Talk where Gates said vaccines reduce child mortality → which reduces birth rates → which reduces population growth. He was describing a well-documented demographic transition. He was arguing FOR vaccination as a way to stabilize population, not reduce it through death. Reuters fact-checked this repeatedly.
MYTH
"Gates created COVID-19 in a lab to sell vaccines."
FACT
No credible evidence. BMGF funded EcoHealth Alliance coronavirus research (as did NIH, USAID, and the Pentagon). No evidence any BMGF-funded research created SARS-CoV-2. The lab leak hypothesis (still debated) does not implicate Gates.
MYTH / SEMI-TRUE
"Gates controls the WHO and uses it to impose his agenda."
FACT
He doesn't "control" WHO. But BMGF is the 2nd largest funder (after the US government) and has directed over $5.5B to vaccine-related programs. Academic peer-reviewed research (BMJ, PMC) finds WHO priorities are shaped by BMGF grant conditions. This is structural influence, not conspiracy — but it is real and documented.
MYTH / REAL ISSUE
"Gates is buying up farmland to control the food supply."
FACT
He is factually the largest private farmland owner in the US (275,000 acres). He has NOT publicly stated any intent to control food supply. However: he simultaneously advocates for synthetic food systems, funds agricultural policy in 50+ countries, and holds equity in food tech companies. The concern is structural conflict of interest, not a secret plan — but it is a legitimate policy debate.
CONFIRMED
Gates had a significant relationship with Epstein after his 2008 conviction.
FACT
Confirmed and admitted by Gates. He has apologized, called it a "terrible mistake." The 2026 DoJ document release shows Epstein was embedded in Microsoft's executive circle and helped facilitate Foundation-related fundraising. The full scope of the relationship remains under ongoing scrutiny.

14The Future — In His Own Vision

Gates's "Year Ahead 2026" letter — titled "Optimism with Footnotes" — is the most comprehensive public statement of his worldview as of this writing. The core argument: the world is going backwards on several metrics (child mortality, malnutrition, foreign aid) but the innovation pipeline has never been stronger. His five pillars:

1. AI as the equalizer: Gates believes AI will be the first technology that genuinely reaches the poorest people first — not last. An AI doctor on a cheap smartphone in rural Nigeria is not less capable than the AI doctor used in a New York hospital. This is the opposite of the PC era, where the digital divide deepened inequality.
2. Energy abundance through innovation: Gates is funding nuclear (TerraPower), green hydrogen, and grid storage. He believes climate change is solvable but not through sacrifice — only through making clean energy cheaper than fossil fuels. He is optimistic that the physics works; he is pessimistic about political will.
3. The $200B final chapter: Gates has pledged to give virtually all his remaining wealth to the Foundation before it closes — within 20 years of his death. He calls the Foundation a "sunset institution." It will spend down and close, not become a permanent endowment. He wants the money used now, not managed forever by future trustees.
4. Disease eradication as the legacy: Polio is at the finish line (99%+ reduction). Malaria deaths are down 40% since 2000. He wants malaria eliminated by 2040. These are not aspirational — they are engineering targets with defined milestone plans and funding.
5. The footnote — what keeps him up at night: Bioterrorism enabled by AI. The collapse of USAID. Resurgent measles in the US. The rejection of vaccines in wealthy countries triggering outbreaks in poor ones. And — though he doesn't say it publicly — the Epstein cloud over everything he's built.
