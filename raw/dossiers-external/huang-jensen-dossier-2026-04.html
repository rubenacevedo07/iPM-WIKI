01Who Is Jensen Huang

Jensen Huang is the co-founder, president, and CEO of NVIDIA — the company that accidentally became the most important infrastructure provider of the AI era, and in doing so made Huang one of the wealthiest and most strategically powerful individuals on Earth. He has been CEO since the company's founding in 1993: a 33-year tenure that makes him one of the longest-serving CEOs of any major technology company in history.

Born Jen-Hsun Huang in Tainan, Taiwan in 1963, he moved to Thailand at age 5 and to the United States at age 9. His parents, believing they were sending him to a prestigious boarding school, placed him in what turned out to be a Kentucky reform school for troubled youth. Huang has spoken about the experience with characteristic equanimity — he learned to be self-reliant, to adapt fast, and to not complain. He washed dishes and bussed tables at Denny's to pay his way. He earned a BS in Electrical Engineering from Oregon State University and an MS from Stanford. He worked at AMD and LSI Logic before co-founding NVIDIA at 30.

The leather jacket is not marketing. He has worn the same style of black leather jacket to every keynote for decades. It is the closest thing Silicon Valley has to a papal vestment — a signal of identity so consistent it has become a cultural symbol. At GTC 2026, he stood on stage in the SAP Center in San Jose in front of 25,000 people and declared: "The agentic AI inflection point has arrived."

02NVIDIA — How It Was Built

NVIDIA was founded at a Denny's restaurant on Berryessa Road in San Jose on April 5, 1993. Huang, Chris Malachowsky, and Curtis Priem — all engineers in their early 30s — sketched out a company focused on 3D graphics chips for video games. The founding insight was architectural: the future of computing would require massively parallel processing, not faster sequential CPUs. They were right by 30 years.

  • 1993NVIDIA founded at Denny's. Three engineers, one thesis: parallel processing for graphics. First investor: Sequoia Capital ($20M seed).
  • 1995First product, NV1, ships — a disaster commercially. Nearly bankrupt. Huang makes a critical call: bet everything on the next chip, NV3. Sega partnership collapses but the chip survives.
  • 1999NVIDIA IPO ($12/share). Same year: launches the GeForce 256 — the world's first GPU (Graphics Processing Unit). Huang coined the term "GPU." The category didn't exist before NVIDIA named it.
  • 2002Buys 3dfx Interactive (main competitor) for $70M. Consolidates PC graphics market. By mid-2000s: dominant in gaming GPUs, destroying ATI and 3dfx.
  • 2006CUDA launches — the most consequential product decision in NVIDIA's history. CUDA is a parallel computing platform that lets programmers use GPU power for general-purpose computing. Almost nobody notices. CUDA will later become the entire moat.
  • 2012AlexNet — a deep neural network — wins the ImageNet competition by a massive margin using two NVIDIA GTX 580 GPUs running CUDA. The AI research community converts to NVIDIA overnight. Huang sees it immediately and pivots the company's strategy toward AI.
  • 2016First DGX-1 AI supercomputer shipped. First customer: Elon Musk / OpenAI. Huang delivers it personally to OpenAI's San Francisco office. The symbolic handoff of the AI future.
  • 2020COVID accelerates cloud and AI spending. NVIDIA acquires Mellanox (networking) for $6.9B. Attempted $40B acquisition of Arm Holdings — blocked by regulators (FTC, EU) in 2022.
  • 2022ChatGPT launches (November). Within weeks, every hyperscaler (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) begins ordering H100 GPUs in massive quantities. NVIDIA backlog explodes. Revenue goes vertical.
  • 2023NVIDIA market cap crosses $1T. Revenue triples year-over-year. H100 chips selling at $30,000+ each; delivery wait times 6–12 months. Huang becomes one of the 10 richest people on Earth.
  • 2024NVIDIA briefly becomes the world's most valuable company ($3.3T peak), overtaking Apple and Microsoft. Blackwell (B100/B200) architecture ships — 4× faster than H100 on AI inference.
  • 2025Revenue reaches $130.5B for FY2025 — up from $26B in FY2023. Data center revenue: $115B. Q4 FY2026: $68.1B in a single quarter. Rubin architecture (next gen) announced at GTC 2026.
03The CUDA Moat — Why NVIDIA Is Irreplaceable

The hardware — the H100, B200 — is remarkable. But the real moat is CUDA. CUDA (Compute Unified Device Architecture) is a software platform that lets developers write programs that run on NVIDIA GPUs. Launched in 2006, almost entirely ignored at the time, it has now been in continuous development for 20 years and has accumulated an ecosystem that competitors cannot replicate in less than a decade, possibly ever.

Every major AI framework — PyTorch, TensorFlow, JAX — is optimized first and primarily for CUDA. Every AI researcher learns on CUDA. Every cloud provider (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) builds CUDA-accelerated instances. There are millions of lines of production AI code written specifically for CUDA. Switching to AMD's ROCm or Intel's oneAPI means rewriting and re-optimizing all of it. The lock-in is not contractual — it is technical, educational, and cultural. It is the deepest software moat in modern technology.

05Politics & Geopolitics — The China Problem

Jensen Huang is the most geopolitically exposed CEO in Silicon Valley. NVIDIA sits at the exact intersection of the US-China technology war: its chips are the primary input for AI development globally — and China wants them desperately. The US government wants to deny them. Huang must navigate both without destroying his company.

China represented approximately 17% of NVIDIA's revenue before export controls. The Biden administration's chip export restrictions (October 2022, tightened 2023) forced NVIDIA to create downgraded "China-compliant" chips (H800, A800) — which China then sought workarounds for. The Trump administration in December 2025 cleared NVIDIA to sell its second-most-powerful chip to China — then negotiated a deal in which NVIDIA and AMD agreed to share 15% of China chip sale revenues with the US government. An arrangement with no historical precedent.

The CUDA ecosystem is the insurance policy against this — rewriting everything to run on custom silicon would cost billions and take years.
08Physical AI — Robotics, Autonomous Systems & the Next Wave

At GTC 2026, Huang declared the arrival of a new era: "Physical AI" — AI that operates in the real world, not just in software. His thesis: after language models (reasoning through text) and agentic AI (AI that takes actions in software), the next wave is AI that controls physical systems — robots, factories, autonomous vehicles, surgical systems, warehouses.

Huang at GTC 2026: "AI data center revenue will reach $1 trillion by 2028 — a market so large it fits every competitor. The question is not whether there will be demand for compute. The question is whether the physical world is programmable. We believe it is. We call this Physical AI."

The Taiwan risk is the most existential scenario — not in probability but in consequence. NVIDIA designs all its chips but manufactures 100% of them at TSMC in Taiwan. A Chinese military action against Taiwan would immediately halt NVIDIA's production entirely. There is no backup fab. This is a known fragility; NVIDIA has lobbied for TSMC's Arizona expansion but advanced node production there remains years away at meaningful scale.

The AI capex pullback risk is real but often overstated. If Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta simultaneously reduce AI infrastructure spending — triggered by an AI winter, a recession, or a breakthrough in efficiency — NVIDIA's revenue would drop sharply. The DeepSeek moment (January 2026, Chinese AI lab achieving GPT-4-level performance at a fraction of the compute cost) caused a $600B single-day market cap drop for NVIDIA — the largest in market history. It recovered. But it demonstrated the fragility of valuation assumptions built on perpetual compute demand growth.

11The Future — Jensen Huang's Own Vision

Huang's world view, assembled from GTC 2026 keynote, Davos interviews, and public statements, has five interconnected theses:

1. The $1 Trillion AI Factory Economy: "AI data center revenue will reach $1 trillion by 2028." Huang frames AI clusters not as computers but as factories that manufacture intelligence — producing "tokens" (units of AI output) the way oil refineries produce barrels. Every enterprise, every government, every nation needs its own AI factory. NVIDIA sells the factory floor.
2. Inference is the New Gold Rush: The first wave (2022–2024) was about training large models. The next wave — already here — is inference: running models billions of times per day at scale. "The more you use AI, the more compute you need. Inference demand has no ceiling." Blackwell and Rubin are inference-optimized architectures.
3. Physical AI Transforms the Material World: "Software has eaten the world. Now AI will eat physics." Robots, factories, vehicles, warehouses — all will run on AI trained in simulation (Omniverse) and deployed on NVIDIA hardware. Huang believes the physical world will be reprogrammed the way the digital world was reprogrammed by software in the 1990s.
4. Every Country Needs Sovereign AI: "A country that doesn't have its own AI infrastructure will be colonized — digitally." Huang is selling the idea that national AI sovereignty requires owning your own compute stack. He is simultaneously selling that compute stack. It is the most effective enterprise sales pitch in technology history.
5. Humans + AI = Amplification, Not Replacement: Unlike Bill Gates ("humans won't be needed for most things"), Huang's message is subtly different: "AI is the most powerful tool humanity has ever built. Every person with access to AI becomes more capable." He frames NVIDIA not as building a replacement for human intelligence but as building an amplifier. Whether this distinction holds as physical AI + robotics matures is the defining question of the next decade.
