# IMF World Economic Outlook — April 2026
**Source URLs:**
- https://www.imf.org/en/publications/weo/issues/2026/04/14/world-economic-outlook-april-2026
- https://www.libertify.com/interactive-library/imf-world-economic-outlook-2026/

## Global Growth Forecast
- WEO 2026 projects global growth ~3.3% for 2026 and 3.2% for 2027 — slight upward revision vs October 2025
- Growth driven by US and India; eurozone and China on weaker trajectories; narrative is "moderate but resilient growth"
- Global inflation continues moderating but disinflation is uneven; some EMs still face persistent inflation pressures

## Key Risks Identified
- Escalation of trade tensions and geoeconomic fragmentation (tariffs, export controls, supply chain reconfiguration)
- Financial risks from higher-for-longer rates especially in high public/corporate debt economies
- Real estate and banking sector vulnerabilities in certain economies; climate risks that can cause food and energy price shocks

## Country-Specific Warnings (top 5)
- United States: relatively strong growth but elevated public debt; need for credible medium-term fiscal consolidation
- China: structural slowdown linked to demographics, productivity, and real estate correction; risk that insufficient or misdirected stimulus prolongs weakness
- Eurozone: low potential growth and internal divergences; banking and real estate vulnerabilities in some countries
- Indebted emerging markets: exposure to rate and exchange rate shocks especially where external currency debt is high
- Low-income economies: debt crisis risk and development setbacks without strengthened debt relief and concessional financing access

## Commodity Outlook
- WEO projects relatively contained energy prices in baseline scenario but identifies upside risk from intensifying Middle East conflicts and maritime chokepoint problems
- Food and agricultural commodities: vulnerable to climate shocks and trade restrictions

## Dollar / Reserve Currency Notes
- Dollar remains dominant reserve and invoicing currency despite de-dollarization debate; adjustments in other currency shares are gradual
- Sharp changes in US financial conditions have significant spillover effects on global financing especially in emerging markets

## Policy Recommendations
- Three pillars: credible fiscal consolidation, structural reforms for productivity, multilateral cooperation to limit fragmentation damage
- Central banks should maintain data-dependent approach; avoid both premature easing and excessive tightening
- Emphasis on investment in digitalisation, human capital, AI adaptation to capture productivity gains and sustain growth
