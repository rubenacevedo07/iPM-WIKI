## Sanctions & Enforcement
- G7/EU oil price cap mechanism remains in place with formal limits on price of Urals for services rendered by G7/EU entities
- Western authorities have adjusted guidelines to strengthen compliance: more documentation required on cargo prices and routes
- US and EU tightened sanctions against shipping companies, insurers, and financial entities facilitating price cap violations — including blacklisting and restrictions on Western financial system access
- Spot inspections increased; some cargo detentions/holds on suspected violations

## Tanker Fleet & Logistics
- Active "dark fleet" of tankers under opaque flags with difficult-to-trace insurers and owners — used to circumvent price caps and sanctions via ship-to-ship transfers and documentation changes
- Estimates: hundreds of vessels involved, sufficient to move significant volume of Russian exports but at higher costs and risks
- Shadow fleet capacity constrains but does not eliminate Russia's ability to monetise oil exports

## India / China Purchases
- India and China remain primary buyers of Russian crude
- Bloomberg reporting: India may reduce purchases from ~1.2M b/d to ~0.4–0.5M b/d by April 2026 — driven by political pressure, less attractive discounts, or supplier diversification
- China maintains significant purchases via sea and pipeline; attentive to Western regulatory pressure on shipping and insurance
- Urals discount vs Brent has narrowed from initial post-invasion levels but still reflects sanctions risk and logistics costs

## Market Impact
- Brent spread: Urals discount vs Brent still reflects "Russia discount" vs comparable crudes
- Spread dynamics influenced by: OPEC+ policy, shadow fleet capacity, India/China willingness to absorb Russian crude in exchange for discounts
- If India follows through on purchase reduction, Russia would need to find alternative buyers or cut production
