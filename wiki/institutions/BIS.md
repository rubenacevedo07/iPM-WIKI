# Bank for International Settlements

## Mandate and Tools
"Central bank of central banks." Based in Basel, Switzerland. BIS does not set rates — it coordinates, researches, and sets standards. Key functions: (1) Basel III/IV capital adequacy standards for global banks, (2) BIS Quarterly Review — the best free macro intelligence publication in the world, (3) CPMI (payments/settlement oversight), (4) FSB (Financial Stability Board) secretariat, (5) FX reserve management for 60+ central banks.

## Internal Structure
- General Manager: Agustín Carstens (to 2026)
- Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS)
- ~700 staff, 63 member central banks
- Investment of ~$300B in central bank reserves

## Current Regime
*(Knowledge-based · April 2026)*

BIS latest quarterly signal (Q4 2025): global financial system under three simultaneous stresses — elevated public debt, persistent inflation above targets, geopolitical fragmentation increasing. Key BIS warning: fiscal dominance risk (governments forcing central banks to monetize debt) is rising. USD funding markets fragile — FX swap lines are the backstop.

## Relationships
- Partners with all major central banks (Fed, ECB, PBOC, BOJ, BOE)
- IMF→Partners→Federal Reserve (via BIS coordination node)
- CPMI oversight of Swift, CLS, TARGET2, Fedwire

## Recent Moves
- Q1 2026 BIS Quarterly: flagged tariff shock as systemic risk multiplier — supply chain disruption + inflation persistence + credit tightening = compounding stress
- 2025: Basel IV implementation timeline extended — bank lobbying secured 2028 full implementation
- 2024: BIS Innovation Hub launched CBDC project with 9 central banks

## Narrative Shift
BIS has become more explicitly political in its warnings — unprecedented language about "fiscal dominance" and "geopolitical fragmentation" in official publications. This signals that central bank consensus is that the macro regime has fundamentally shifted post-2022.

## Market Impact Channels
- BIS Quarterly Review = high-value intelligence source. Markets re-price on BIS warnings about specific sectors (commercial real estate, leveraged loans, EM debt).
- Basel capital requirements = structural constraint on bank leverage → credit availability globally.
- BIS FX intervention coordination: when USD funding stress spikes (March 2020, Sept 2022), BIS-coordinated swap lines prevent freeze.
