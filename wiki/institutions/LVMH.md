# LVMH

## Mandate and Tools
World's largest luxury group. Portfolio: Louis Vuitton, Dior, Givenchy, Moët & Chandon, Hennessy, Bulgari, TAG Heuer, Sephora, DFS duty-free, Le Bon Marché. Revenue: ~€85B (2025). Listed: Euronext Paris (MC.PA). Controlled by Bernard Arnault (family ~47% voting control) — world's richest European and #3-4 globally by wealth.

## Internal Structure
- Chairman & CEO: Bernard Arnault — the defining personality of global luxury
- HQ: Paris, 75008
- ~215,000 employees globally
- 75+ houses across wine/spirits, fashion/leather, perfumes/cosmetics, watches/jewelry, selective retail
- Louis Vuitton alone: ~€25B+ revenue, highest margin luxury brand globally

## Current Regime
*(Knowledge-based · April 2026)*

LVMH is the single best proxy for Chinese consumer aspirations and global wealth creation. Its revenue composition:
- China + HK + Macau: ~30% of group revenue historically (now ~25% after post-COVID rebalancing)
- US: ~25% — high-income consumers, resilient
- Europe: ~20% — tourist-driven (Chinese, US, Middle Eastern buyers in Paris/Milan)
- Japan: ~10% — yen weakness = Japanese buying domestic, foreigners buying in Japan

**Current pressure:** Chinese luxury demand has normalized post-COVID revenge-spending boom. Beijing's "common prosperity" campaign suppressed conspicuous luxury consumption. US consumer resilience is the compensating factor. Japan became a luxury tourism destination due to weak yen.

**Tariff risk:** US tariffs on French goods (wine, cognac, luxury) = direct Hennessy/LVMH revenue hit. Cognac = 80% of French cognac exports go to US — a Trump tariff weapon vs France.

## Relationships
- Bernard Arnault: Controlling shareholder + political influence in France and globally
- Arnault-Macron relationship: Complex — Arnault moved family assets to Belgium (2012 Hollande wealth tax), but LVMH is a French national champion Macron actively protects
- LVMH vs Kering/Hermès: Competitive dynamic with Gucci (Kering) and Hermès (Birkin scarcity model)

## Recent Moves
- 2026-Q1: Q1 revenue growth slowing — China normalization vs US resilience
- 2026-Q1: Cognac exports to US threatened by Trump tariff escalation (retaliation for EU tariffs on US goods)
- 2026-Q1: LVMH Ventures investing in AI-personalization for retail (Sephora, Louis Vuitton)
- 2025: MC.PA -15% from peak — China demand normalization disappointment
- 2025: Arnault family succession question — 5 children in management, no clear heir

## Narrative Shift
From "China growth machine" (2015-2023) to "China normalization + US resilience story" (2024-2026). Arnault has been publicly cautious about China — unusual for a CEO who built his empire on China's middle-class aspiration. The Beijing "common prosperity" signal was taken seriously at LVMH. The company is quietly diversifying its China dependency via Japan/Korea expansion.

## Market Impact Channels
- **MC.PA:** Primary indicator of Chinese consumer confidence. Better predictor of China's real economic sentiment than official PMI data. "LVMH China sales" is a quarterly market-moving event.
- **CAC 40:** LVMH is ~10-12% of CAC 40 weight — single largest constituent. CAC 40 performance = partially LVMH.
- **EUR/USD:** LVMH revenues in USD/CNY/JPY, costs in EUR. EUR weakness = LVMH margin tailwind.
- **Cognac / wine tariffs:** US tariff on French cognac = direct hit to Hennessy (~€3B revenue). Macro headline risk.
- **Wealth effect:** LVMH tracks global UHNWI (ultra-high net worth) creation more than GDP. Finance/tech billionaire wealth → LVMH demand.
