# IMP Oracle System — 20 Machines + Brier Scoring

## Summary
IMP tracks 20 real-world analysts under fictional machine names. Records their public predictions. Scores weekly with the Brier rule. Publishes calibrated rankings. Internal mapping (real identity → machine name) is never exposed.

## The Three-Way Alpha Signal
The core commercial differentiator. When these three diverge, that gap is the product.

| Signal | Source | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| Human consensus | Polymarket CLOB midpoint (real USDC) | What the crowd believes, priced in |
| AI machine ensemble | Weighted average of 20 machines by domain Brier score | What calibrated AI thinks |
| Individual machine | Per-machine prediction with confidence band | Who specifically called this |
| **Divergence signal** | AI ensemble vs Polymarket > 20 points | **Alpha — information asymmetry** |
| Machine disagreement | Internal spread between 20 machines | The disagreement itself is signal |

> "When IMP's machine ensemble says 72% and Polymarket says 51% — that 21-point divergence is the product."

## Brier Scoring — Why It Matters
`Brier score = (probability − outcome)²` — lower is better. Perfect = 0. Random = 0.25.

Unlike hit-rate metrics, Brier penalizes overconfidence. A machine that says 90% and is right 70% of the time scores **worse** than one that says 70% and is right 70% of the time. No competitor uses it. Most analyst tracking uses hit rate, which rewards confidence over calibration.

## AI Analyst Avatars
Each machine becomes a Claude-powered reasoning model built from the public record of its real-world source.

Process:
1. Collect every public prediction, essay, transcript, interview from real analyst
2. Extract reasoning framework: priors, methodology, domain expertise, risk appetite
3. Encode as IdeologyProfile 7-axis (−10 to +10) in IMP graph
4. Construct avatar system prompt = framework priors + domain Brier scores + calibration curve
5. Avatars disagree with each other — the disagreement is signal, not noise
