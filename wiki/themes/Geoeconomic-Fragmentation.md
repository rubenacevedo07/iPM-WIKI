# Geoeconomic Fragmentation & Trade Wars

## Strategic Summary

The post-1990 liberal trade order — built on comparative advantage, WTO dispute resolution, and the assumption that economic interdependence reduces conflict — is fracturing. The fracture runs along three axes simultaneously: tariffs (US vs China and EU), sanctions (Russia oil, Iran, tech), and export controls (AI chips, semiconductor tools). Together they are reorganising global supply chains, investment flows, and capital markets around geopolitical blocs rather than economic efficiency.

The IMF WEO April 2026 identifies geoeconomic fragmentation as the top structural risk to global growth — estimating that full fragmentation into two blocs could reduce global GDP by 2-7% permanently. The world is not yet at full fragmentation, but the trajectory is clearly toward more, not less.

## Key Dimensions

**Tariffs (US vs China, EU, others)**
- Section 301 tariffs on China maintained and expanding — now covering broad basket of goods with new investigations underway
- March 2026: US opened trade investigations vs 16 countries including EU — potential new tariff wave summer 2026
- Tariffs now explicitly framed as industrial policy + national security tools, not just trade negotiation leverage
- See [[../market-impact/Trump-Trade-War-Markets]] for market impact

**Sanctions (Russia oil, Iran, tech)**
- Russia oil price cap mechanism: G7/EU cap at $60/barrel; shadow fleet operational to circumvent
- Iran: comprehensive sanctions regime; nuclear programme uncertainty; IRGC controls Hormuz
- Tech sanctions: entity lists, export controls — now covering AI chips, semiconductor tools, cloud services
- See [[Energy-Chokepoints-War]] for energy sanctions specifically

**Export controls (AI chips, tools)**
- US BIS rules restrict frontier GPU exports to China
- Netherlands/Japan joined to restrict ASML EUV shipments
- Result: two diverging AI ecosystems — US-aligned and China-constrained
- See [[AI-Supply-Chain-Export-Controls]] for full analysis

**Supply chain realignment (friendshoring, nearshoring)**
- Companies relocating manufacturing away from China to "trusted" geographies
- Mexico: nearshoring from China for US market access
- Vietnam, India, Thailand: electronics and light manufacturing
- Friendshoring vocabulary now standard in Fortune 500 earnings calls — structural, not temporary

## Global Institutions View

**IMF WEO April 2026:** Global growth 3.3% but top risk is fragmentation. Could reduce global GDP 2-7% if full bloc formation occurs. Recommends multilateral cooperation — acknowledges this is increasingly difficult.

**BIS:** Cross-border capital flows show early signs of geopolitical alignment — US-allied economies maintaining/increasing flows with each other while reducing China exposure.

**WTO:** Dispute resolution effectively paralysed — appellate body still non-functional. Countries increasingly using unilateral measures instead of multilateral channels.

## Regional Impact

**US:** Growth relatively strong (AI investment boom, reshoring manufacturing). But consumers pay tariff costs through higher prices — inflationary pressure that complicates Fed rate cuts. Long-term: fiscal deficit and debt sustainability concerns.

**EU:** Caught between US and China. Cannot afford to fully decouple from either. Under pressure to "de-risk" from China while maintaining trade. Strategic autonomy rhetoric vs economic reality. Energy vulnerability (post-Russia) = structural weakness.

**China:** Trade war hitting export model. Response: "dual circulation" (boost domestic demand) + tech self-sufficiency drive. GDP slowing structurally (demographics, property, productivity). Still running large trade surpluses but with less access to US technology.

**Emerging Markets:** Biggest victims of fragmentation. Supply chain shifts create winners (Vietnam, Mexico, India) and losers (countries dependent on stable trade routes and access to both US and China). Dollar debt exposure + higher-for-longer US rates = financial stress.

## Market Impact Summary

- **Trade volumes:** Global trade growth slowing; intra-bloc trade rising; inter-bloc trade falling
- **Investment flows:** FDI relocating to "friendly" geographies; China FDI inflows declining sharply
- **FX:** Dollar remains dominant but fragmentation reduces its reach; yuan internationalisation accelerating but limited
- **Risk premia:** Geopolitical risk premium embedded in equities, credit, and commodities — historically unprecedented in peacetime
