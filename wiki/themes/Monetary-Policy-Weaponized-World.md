# Monetary Policy in a Weaponized World

## Strategic Summary

Central banks were designed to manage domestic inflation and employment in a stable, globalised world where supply chains were efficient and geopolitical shocks were occasional. That world is gone. Powell and Lagarde now operate in an environment where energy prices are set by Iranian generals and Yemeni militias, where tariffs are industrial policy, and where supply chain disruption is structural rather than temporary. Their frameworks — built on the assumption that shocks are transitory — are straining against a reality where they are not.

The IMP signal: the Fed-ECB ideology divergence (EUR/USD signal = 4.5/10) tells you the structural direction. But geopolitical shocks are the volatility layer on top. When a chokepoint flares, it doesn't change the structural signal — it amplifies or disrupts it temporarily.

## Fed Narrative (2025–2026)

**2022–2023: "Transitory" → "We were wrong"**
The Fed's insistence that post-COVID inflation was transitory delayed tightening. The correction — 525bps of hikes in 18 months — was the fastest in 40 years.

**2024–2025: "Higher for longer"**
Having overcorrected the "transitory" mistake, the Fed adopted a deliberately data-dependent posture. Cuts would only come when inflation was sustainably at 2%. This became the dominant narrative.

**2026: "No hurry to cut"**
March 2026 FOMC: rates held at 4.5–4.75%. Dot plot revised hawkish — fewer 2026 cuts. Inflation projections revised UP to 2.7%. Powell explicitly flags tariff and energy price shocks as upside inflation risks. The geopolitical environment is now a direct input into Fed forward guidance.

Key structural tension: Trump (AuthScore 7.5) wants rate cuts for political reasons. Powell (AuthScore 0.0) is constitutionally resistant to political pressure. This gap — 7.5 points on AuthScore — is the Fed independence risk premium embedded in dollar assets.

## ECB Narrative (2025–2026)

**2022–2024: Late to hike, fast to catch up**
The ECB started hiking later than the Fed but moved aggressively. Peak rate reached with less labour market buffer than the US — eurozone growth was weaker throughout.

**2025: Peak → small cut**
ECB completed hiking cycle and made a small precautionary cut as inflation approached target and growth remained weak.

**2026: Watchful pause**
Current stance: rates held at slightly restrictive level. Inflation "around 2% target." Growth weak but not recessionary. Data-dependent, meeting by meeting. No commitment on timing of next cut.

Key structural challenge: eurozone is a net energy importer. Energy shocks are stagflationary — they raise prices AND reduce growth simultaneously. The ECB cannot simply hike to fight energy inflation without worsening the recession risk. The Fed faces this less acutely because the US is now a net energy exporter.

## Interaction with Geopolitics

**Energy shocks → inflation → forward guidance delay**
Every chokepoint flare that raises oil prices enters headline CPI within weeks. This shifts "will they cut this meeting?" to "will they cut this year?" for both Fed and ECB. The market reads the delay and re-prices the curve.

**Trade tensions → supply chain costs → core inflation**
Tariffs raise the price of imported goods — initially one-time, potentially persistent if supply chains don't adjust. Powell's explicit concern: if tariff shocks anchor inflation expectations higher, rate cuts become impossible without credibility damage.

**Financial stability spillovers**
Higher-for-longer US rates stress EM dollar borrowers, corporate leveraged buyouts, and commercial real estate globally. If a financial stability event occurs — major bank failure, EM debt crisis — both Fed and ECB face the classic dilemma: cut for stability or hold for inflation.

## Forward Guidance & Market Pricing

Current market read (April 2026):
- Fed: 1-2 cuts priced for 2026, first cut Q3/Q4 2026 at earliest
- ECB: 1-2 cuts priced, earlier than Fed given weaker eurozone growth
- Curve: flat/inverted in US, slightly steeper in EU
- USD: bid on rate differential and safe-haven demand
- EUR/USD: structurally below long-term average; directional bias USD-positive

Key risk to consensus: if US growth disappoints (tariff drag + consumer weakness) while inflation falls faster than expected → Fed cuts faster → USD weakens → EUR/USD recovery
