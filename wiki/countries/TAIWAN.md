# TAIWAN

## Strategic Summary
Taiwan produces 90%+ of the world's advanced semiconductors (sub-5nm) through TSMC. Its geographic position in the Taiwan Strait — the most contested waterway in the Indo-Pacific — makes it simultaneously the single most important chokepoint for the global AI and electronics supply chain and the most likely flashpoint for US-China military conflict. Taiwan's survival strategy is "silicon shield" deterrence: make itself so economically indispensable that invasion becomes globally catastrophic.

## Power Profile
- **DB ID:** 151
- **Military capacity:** Seeded. Asymmetric defence posture. ~170K active military. US arms sales (F-16s, Harpoon missiles, HIMARS). Porcupine strategy — focus on coastal/urban denial vs conventional defence.
- **Economic weight:** ~$800B GDP. Semiconductors = 35%+ of exports. TSMC market cap ~$800B.
- **Information power:** High — US intelligence sharing, Five Eyes adjacent.
- **Diplomatic influence:** Limited formal recognition (12 UN members) but de facto relations with 60+ countries via trade offices.
- **Chokepoint exposure:** Taiwan Strait — ClosureRiskScore=8/10. Controlling both shores requires controlling Taiwan and mainland China simultaneously.
- **Veto points:** TSMC Jade Mountain protocol — self-destruction contingency renders fabs inoperable. This is the ultimate deterrence beyond military capability.

## Domestic Pressures
- DPP vs KMT divide on cross-strait relations — Lai Ching-te more confrontational than predecessor Tsai
- Youth demographic declining — defense recruitment challenges
- Economic dependency on China (25%+ of exports) creates business community pressure for status quo
- US pressure to increase defense spending (currently ~2.5% GDP)

## External Alignments
- US: Taiwan Relations Act = de facto security guarantee. Arms sales. CHIPS Act incentivizes TSMC Arizona.
- Japan: Joint defense planning. TSMC Kumamoto fab. Malacca dependency shared.
- South Korea: Samsung competitor but shared semiconductor supply chain vulnerability
- China: "One country, two systems" offer permanently rejected. Economic leverage via trade dependency.

## Sanctions and Legal Constraints
- TSMC restricted from selling advanced chips (below 5nm equivalent) to Chinese customers per US BIS export controls
- Taiwan semiconductor equipment imports from ASML require US/Dutch government licenses

## Narrative Trajectory
*(Updated 2026-04-21 · knowledge-based)*

Lai Ching-te's presidency has raised cross-strait tension incrementally. His willingness to use the word "independence" vs predecessor Tsai's deliberate ambiguity is a red line signal to Beijing. Taiwan's military has absorbed lessons from Ukraine — shift toward asymmetric/drone warfare, longer-range missiles, civilian defense. TSMC Arizona (Fab 21, 4nm) is producing but not at scale — the silicon shield logic still holds for 2026-2027. US commitment to Taiwan's defence remains the central deterrence variable.
