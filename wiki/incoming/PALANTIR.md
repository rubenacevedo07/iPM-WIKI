## Summary

Palantir Technologies is an American data analytics and AI company that has evolved from a secretive post-9/11 intelligence contractor into the most politically significant and ideologically explicit technology company in the United States. Co-founded in 2003 by Peter Thiel, Alex Karp, and two others — with seed funding from In-Q-Tel (the CIA's venture arm) — Palantir built its business on the premise that software could solve intelligence and military problems that traditional contractors could not. Twenty years later, it is the primary technological infrastructure of US mass deportation, a core component of the US and NATO military targeting apparatus, an active participant in Israeli military operations in Gaza, and the author of what critics have called a "technofascist" ideological manifesto for Western civilizational hard power.

Palantir's rise under Trump 2.0 is not incidental. The company's co-founder chairs its board; its co-founder funded the Vice President's Senate campaign; its ideology aligns with the administration's; and it has harvested more than $1.3 billion in federal contracts since January 2025.

## Mandate and Ideology

### The Karp Doctrine

Palantir's stated mission, as crystallized in *The Technological Republic* (2025), is that Western liberal democracy requires "hard power" built on software to survive against authoritarian adversaries. The company has "chosen sides" — a phrase from its 2020 IPO filing — and that side is the US national security state and its allies.

Key tenets of the published manifesto (22-point X thread, April 2026):
- Silicon Valley has a "moral debt" to America and an "affirmative obligation" to participate in national defense
- AI weapons will be built; the only question is who builds them
- Hard power — not soft power — determines civilizational survival
- Western cultures have produced "vital advances" while others "remain dysfunctional and regressive" (civilizational hierarchy framing)
- National service and potentially the draft should be reconsidered
- Tech companies must play a role in addressing violent crime

The manifesto generated 21M+ X impressions and simultaneous condemnation from philosophers, parliamentarians, human rights groups, and foreign ministers. Critics labeled it "technofascism" (Mark Coeckelbergh), an "AI-driven threat to humanity's existence" (Yanis Varoufakis), and "as subtle as a MAGA hat" (Tech Policy Press).

## Current Regime: Trump 2.0 Contract Surge

Since Trump's inauguration (January 20, 2025), Palantir has been awarded contracts with 14+ federal agencies including Defense, Homeland Security, Treasury, State, HHS, Veterans Affairs, Energy, Transportation, Justice, Agriculture, HUD, Commerce, NASA, GSA, and the US International Development Finance Corporation.

**Total value (January 2025–April 2026):** $1.3B+

**Marquee contracts:**
- **Maven Smart System:** Contract ceiling raised from $480M to $1.275B through 2029 (May 2025). 20,000+ users across 35 military entities.
- **NATO MSS NATO:** Acquired March 25, 2025 by NATO Communications and Information Agency for Allied Command Operations. Fastest contract in NATO history (six months from requirement to acquisition).
- **ImmigrationOS:** $30M, April 2025. ICE. Sole-source justification. Prototype delivered September 2025.
- **DHS/ICE umbrella:** $1B contract signed February 2026 for software and services supporting immigration enforcement.
- **HHS:** $405M (former Palantir engineer serves as HHS CIO).
- **UK Ministry of Defence:** £750M ($1B+) strategic partnership over five years — Palantir's first non-US contract exceeding $1B.

## Relationships

**Peter Thiel (co-founder/chairman):** The architect. See above and `wiki/actors/THIEL-Peter.md`.

**CIA / In-Q-Tel (founding):** Palantir was seeded by the CIA's venture arm. Built early business on post-9/11 intelligence contracts. The CIA origin story — and the company's subsequent role in US surveillance infrastructure — shapes its culture and its critics' framing.

**US DoD (Maven):** Primary military client. Deep institutional dependency created by Maven's user base and integration with combatant commands. Palantir CTO commissioned as Army Reserve officer.

**DHS / ICE:** Relationship since 2013. ICM and ImmigrationOS make Palantir "mission-critical" to the mass deportation apparatus. ICE's own justification document states Palantir is "the only source" capable of delivering these capabilities.

**NATO:** MSS NATO acquisition March 2025 marks the first operational AI targeting platform adopted at the Alliance level. Geopolitically significant: NATO ties itself to US private infrastructure at the moment Trump is pressuring European capitals on burden-sharing.

**Ukraine Ministry of Defense:** Palantir provides battlefield intelligence, targeting, war crimes documentation, and landmine detection support. Ukraine has become, per multiple analysts, "an AI war lab" for Palantir's systems. Karp: Palantir is "responsible for most of the targeting in Ukraine."

**IDF / Israel Ministry of Defense:** "Strategic partnership" announced January 2024. Palantir expanded operations in Gaza and West Bank. Permanent desk at US Civil-Military Coordination Center (CMCC), Kiryat Gat. UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese found "reasonable grounds to believe" Palantir provided AI infrastructure used in the Gaza campaign. Palantir denies involvement in specific IDF targeting systems (Lavender, Gospel) while confirming it "supports Israel" and is "proud of the work we do in Israel."

**Unit 8200 (Israel):** Palantir has recruited heavily from the Israeli military's cyber-intelligence wing. Their expertise shapes Palantir's AI capabilities.

## Narrative Shift

- **2003–2013 ("Secretive CIA contractor"):** Palantir's founding era. Quasi-mythological secrecy; the company was described as "the startup that could destroy privacy" and "Big Brother's little helper." Known mainly within defense and intelligence circles.
- **2013–2019 ("Government surveillance debate"):** ICE contracts, Edward Snowden era, growing civil liberties scrutiny. First major protests and employee dissent. Karp begins defending the company's mission publicly.
- **2020 ("Chosen sides"):** IPO. The SEC filing's explicit declaration — "We have chosen sides" — marks Palantir's decision to stop apologizing for its mission and start broadcasting it.
- **2025–present ("Unapologetic West defender"):** *The Technological Republic*, the ImmigrationOS deployment, NATO adoption of Maven, and the $1.3B federal contract surge under Trump 2.0 represent Palantir fully operational as an ideological actor, not just a contractor.

## Political Risk Assessment

**EU regulatory exposure:** UK parliamentary scrutiny intensifying (MoD contract, NHS contract, Gaza involvement). European institutional investors divesting. European Commission DSA scrutiny extends to Palantir's data practices.

**Civil liberties litigation:** ACLU, EPIC, Amnesty International, NYU Stern, and others pursuing ICE contract legal challenges and FOIA disclosure. Ongoing risk of injunctions on ImmigrationOS deployment.

**Gaza liability:** UN Special Rapporteur finding creates potential international legal exposure. Norway's Storebrand, University of San Francisco, SF State University, and others have divested PLTR on human rights grounds. UK MPs calling for contract review.

**Conflict of interest/congressional risk:** Detachment 201 (Palantir CTO as Army Reserve LTC), personnel capture at HHS, and the Thiel-Vance-Palantir pipeline create bipartisan oversight risk as the administration's political position evolves.

**Concentration:** A P/E above 230 (2025) prices in substantial political continuity. A 2028 administration change would create immediate contract risk.
