## The Technological Republic — Karp Doctrine

**Publication:** *The Technological Republic: Hard Power, Soft Belief, and the Future of the West*
**Authors:** Alexander C. Karp (CEO, Palantir) & Nicholas W. Zamiska (Head of Corporate Affairs)
**Published:** Early 2025. Reached #1 New York Times Bestseller.
**Manifesto moment:** April 18–20, 2026 — Palantir posted a 22-point X thread summarizing the book, titled "Because we get asked a lot. The Technological Republic, in brief." The post rapidly reached 21M+ views and ignited a global controversy.

---

## Core Claims (the 22 Points — summary)

1. Silicon Valley owes a "moral debt" to the US — engineers have an "affirmative obligation" to participate in national defense.
2. Rebellion against the tyranny of apps — the iPhone as the ceiling of civilization, not its pinnacle.
3. "Free email is not enough" — cultural decadence is only forgiven by nations that deliver growth and security.
4. Limits of soft power exposed — "hard power in this century will be built on software."
5. The question is not whether AI weapons will be built; it is who builds them. Adversaries will not pause for "theatrical debates."
6. National service as universal duty — serious consideration of reinstating the draft.
7. "If a US Marine asks for a better rifle, we should build it; and the same goes for software."
8. Public servants should be better compensated; government can't attract talent at current pay.
9. Silicon Valley must address violent crime.
10. West's resistance to "defining national cultures in the name of inclusivity" is a weakness.
11. Treatment of billionaires and "ruthless exposure of private lives of public figures" drives talent from government.
12. Some cultures "have produced vital advances while others remain dysfunctional and regressive" — civilizational hierarchy framing.
13. Rearmament of Germany and Japan presented as necessary.
14. AI will prove more transformative than nuclear power.
15. Tech companies have a moral duty to develop AI for defense.

**Full book:** 320 pages. Part 3 (chapters 10-14) focuses on Palantir's internal culture.

## Ideological Context / Karp Doctrine

Karp's stated mission for Palantir: help "our enemies wake up scared and go to bed scared." Has previously described Palantir's work as helping to "scare enemies and on occasion, kill them." Described himself as "completely anti-woke" (2025).

The manifesto is the public crystallization of a worldview Karp has held since Palantir's founding — that Western liberal democracy requires hard technological power to survive, and that Silicon Valley's consumer-app focus represents a civilizational failure. The Karp doctrine: moral responsibility + technical capacity + national loyalty = obligation to arm the West.

**Market implication:** The manifesto controversy briefly pressured PLTR stock but analysts noted the political tailwinds under Trump 2.0 more than offset reputational risk in Western markets. EU exposure remains a longer-term risk.
