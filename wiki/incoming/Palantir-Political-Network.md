## II. The Karp Manifesto (Palantir's Ideological Declaration)

### What it is

*The Technological Republic: Hard Power, Soft Belief, and the Future of the West* (2025) — a 320-page book by Palantir CEO Alex Karp and head of corporate affairs Nicholas Zamiska. Reached #1 on the NYT Bestseller list. In April 2026, Palantir distilled it to a 22-point X thread titled "Because we get asked a lot. The Technological Republic, in brief." The post reached 21M+ views.

### What it claims

The manifesto argues that:
- Western liberal democracy is in civilizational crisis because its elites — including Silicon Valley — have retreated into consumerism, cultural pluralism, and soft power while adversaries build hard power
- Technology companies have a moral obligation to participate in national defense, not merely a financial opportunity to do so
- AI weapons will be developed; the ethical question is not whether to build them but who does
- "Some cultures have produced vital advances while others remain dysfunctional and regressive" — a hierarchy of civilizations as policy premise
- National service (potentially including reinstated draft) is a necessary civic institution
- The treatment of billionaires and public figures is driving talent away from government service

### What it justifies

The manifesto provides the ideological framework for:
- Palantir's ICE deportation infrastructure (hard power implementation of immigration enforcement)
- Palantir's IDF targeting support (allied democracy in existential conflict)
- Maven Smart System as DoD targeting backbone (tech obligation to defense)
- Palantir CTO commissioned as Army Reserve officer (blurring contractor/military line as feature, not bug)

### Critical reception

The manifesto divided commentators sharply. Endorsers include Niall Ferguson, the Wall Street Journal, Washington Post, and UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer's government (which The Times of London called the "AI manifesto inspiring Starmer"). Critics include a Belgian philosopher of technology ("technofascism"), former Greek Finance Minister Yanis Varoufakis ("AI-driven threat to humanity's existence"), UK Liberal Democrat MPs, and multiple academic analysts who describe the book as a self-serving corporate wishlist dressed in pseudo-philosophical language.

## III. ICE / DHS Programs: The Deportation Stack

### FALCON / ICM (2013–present)

Palantir built ICE's Investigative Case Management (ICM) system beginning in 2014, based on its proprietary FALCON platform. The system gives every ICE agent network access to federal and private databases: cell location, air travel records, student visa databases (SEVIS), field interview records, and more. Palantir has operated this mission-critical infrastructure through Obama, Biden, and both Trump administrations.

### ImmigrationOS (2025–2027)

In April 2025, ICE awarded Palantir a $30M sole-source contract to build ImmigrationOS — described by ICE as an "urgent" upgrade to ICM. ICE's own justification: Palantir is "the only source" capable of delivery in time; delays would "undercut enforcement mandates outlined in Trump's executive orders."

ImmigrationOS aggregates data from IRS, Social Security, passport records, license plates, and federal databases into a unified targeting interface. Three functions:
1. **Targeting prioritization** — who to remove first (violent criminals, gang members, visa overstays)
2. **Self-deportation tracking** — near real-time visibility on voluntary departures
3. **Removal logistics** — from "identification to removal" in one interface

Prototype delivered September 2025. CNN reported that agents can "approve raids, book arrests, generate legal documents, and route individuals to deportation flights or detention — all from a single interface."

**February 2026:** DHS signs $1B umbrella contract with Palantir for immigration enforcement software and services.

**December 2025:** Palantir wins additional USCIS contract — now operating on both the enforcement (ICE) and benefits (USCIS) sides of immigration.

**Civil liberties concerns:** ACLU, Amnesty International, NYU Stern, and digital rights groups have noted: no public information on accuracy, no independent oversight, errors can cause wrongful detention and deportation, and the system could be expanded to target any American. Thirteen former Palantir employees signed a letter condemning the contracts.

## IV. War Programs

### Ukraine

Since 2022, Palantir has provided Ukraine's military with battlefield intelligence support — initially reported as free of charge. Karp has stated Palantir is "responsible for most of the targeting in Ukraine." The company supports targeting, war crimes evidence collection, and landmine detection. From detection to target strike: "no more than two or three minutes" elapse, per a former Portuguese official shown Palantir's London operations.

Ukraine has become what multiple analysts describe as "an AI war lab" for Palantir's systems — accelerating capability development that feeds back into the Maven Smart System's DoD deployment. In March 2024, Palantir signed an agreement with Ukraine's Ministry of Economy. Ukraine's MoD is working to formalize the legal framework for military software use.

### Maven Smart System (DoD/NATO)

Palantir's commercialized battlefield AI — the central platform for US military AI targeting and intelligence fusion:

- **May 2024:** Initial $480M, 5-year DoD IDIQ contract
- **May 2025:** DOD raises contract ceiling to $1.275B through 2029 — "growing demand" from combatant commands
- **March 25, 2025:** NATO Communications and Information Agency acquires MSS NATO for Allied Command Operations — "one of the most expeditious contracts in NATO history" (six months from requirement to acquisition)
- **Deployment:** 20,000+ users across 35 military entities; CENTCOM, EUCOM, INDOPACOM, NORTHCOM/NORAD, TRANSCOM; Joint Staff
- **Capability:** Reduces targeting data processing from 12 hours to under 1 minute; "20-50 soldiers can analyze data previously requiring hundreds or thousands"

In June 2025, Palantir CTO Shyam Sankar was commissioned as a US Army Reserve lieutenant colonel through Detachment 201, a Pentagon initiative embedding Silicon Valley executives in the military to "inject speed and expertise."

### Gaza / IDF

**January 2024:** Palantir announces "strategic partnership" with Israel's Ministry of Defense for "war-related missions." Board meeting held in Tel Aviv "in solidarity" with Israel.

Documented involvement:
- Permanent desk at US Civil-Military Coordination Center (CMCC), Kiryat Gat (from at least November 2025), providing AI architecture for Gaza aid tracking
- Gaia platform confirmed deployed at CMCC
- Multiple raids in Gaza reportedly used Palantir software (per CEO biography)
- September 2024 Lebanon pager attacks: Israel used Palantir systems

Disputed involvement:
- Palantir denies involvement with IDF's "Lavender," "Gospel," and "Where's Daddy" targeting AI systems
- UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese (June 2025) found "reasonable grounds to believe Palantir provided automatic predictive policing technology, core defence infrastructure, and its AI Platform for real-time battlefield data integration"
- Amnesty International, +972 Magazine, and others allege deeper integration than Palantir acknowledges

**Palantir's position:** "We support Israel. We've chosen to support them because of the appalling events of October 7th." (Spokesperson to Al Jazeera)

## V. The Musk-AfD Connection

### Timeline

- **December 2024:** Musk begins endorsing AfD on X; publishes op-ed in German newspaper urging AfD vote; AfD X engagement increases 8-fold in one week (DFRLab analysis)
- **January 9, 2025:** X Space livestream with AfD leader Alice Weidel. ~200,000 live listeners. Musk tells audience: "People really need to get behind the AfD." Weidel calls Hitler a "communist"; Musk agrees.
- **January 25, 2025:** Musk makes virtual appearance at 4,500-person AfD rally in Eastern Germany: "Fight for a great future for Germany."
- **February 23, 2025:** German Federal Election. AfD finishes second with ~20% of the vote; remains blocked from coalition by all other parties.

### Structural significance

The Musk-AfD intervention is not a Palantir operation, but it is part of the same structural phenomenon. The ideological parallels with the Karp doctrine are direct:
- Civilizational decline narrative: the West (Germany) has been weakened by immigration, pluralism, and elite complacency
- Hard intervention required: only AfD can "save Germany"
- Tech billionaires as legitimate political actors operating outside normal democratic accountability
- Anti-"woke" framing: immigration restriction as civilizational self-preservation

The DFRLab analysis found no systematic X algorithm manipulation — Musk's boost was personal influence, not platform rigging. The European Commission found no DSA violations warranting formal enforcement.

### Risk dimension

Musk has a Tesla factory near Berlin and significant EU business exposure. His AfD campaigning complicated his European business relationships and intensified EU regulatory scrutiny of X. The AfD endorsement also introduced unpredictability into the Thiel network's European positioning — Thiel has historically maintained relationships with European center-right and conservative governments; Musk's alignment with a party Germany's intelligence services classify as a suspected extremist organization is a different posture.
