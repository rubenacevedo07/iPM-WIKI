## II. The Karp Manifesto (Palantir's Ideological Declaration)

### What it is

*The Technological Republic: Hard Power, Soft Belief, and the Future of the West* (2025) — a 320-page book by Palantir CEO Alex Karp and head of corporate affairs Nicholas Zamiska. Reached #1 on the NYT Bestseller list. In April 2026, Palantir distilled it to a 22-point X thread titled "Because we get asked a lot. The Technological Republic, in brief." The post reached 21M+ views.

### What it claims

The manifesto argues that:
- Western liberal democracy is in civilizational crisis because its elites — including Silicon Valley — have retreated into consumerism, cultural pluralism, and soft power while adversaries build hard power
- Technology companies have a moral obligation to participate in national defense, not merely a financial opportunity to do so
- AI weapons will be developed; the ethical question is not whether to build them but who does
- "Some cultures have produced vital advances while others remain dysfunctional and regressive" — a hierarchy of civilizations as policy premise
- National service (potentially including reinstated draft) is a necessary civic institution
- The treatment of billionaires and public figures is driving talent away from government service

### What it justifies

The manifesto provides the ideological framework for:
- Palantir's ICE deportation infrastructure (hard power implementation of immigration enforcement)
- Palantir's IDF targeting support (allied democracy in existential conflict)
- Maven Smart System as DoD targeting backbone (tech obligation to defense)
- Palantir CTO commissioned as Army Reserve officer (blurring contractor/military line as feature, not bug)

### Critical reception

The manifesto divided commentators sharply. Endorsers include Niall Ferguson, the Wall Street Journal, Washington Post, and UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer's government (which The Times of London called the "AI manifesto inspiring Starmer"). Critics include a Belgian philosopher of technology ("technofascism"), former Greek Finance Minister Yanis Varoufakis ("AI-driven threat to humanity's existence"), UK Liberal Democrat MPs, and multiple academic analysts who describe the book as a self-serving corporate wishlist dressed in pseudo-philosophical language.

## III. ICE / DHS Programs: The Deportation Stack

**ICM — Investigative Case Management (2013–present):**
Original FALCON contract (2014) with ICE's Homeland Security Investigations (HSI). Gives every ICE agent access to a network of federal and private databases — cell location, air travel (APIS), student visas (SEVIS), field interview records. Total ICM contract value 2011–2025: $287M.

**ImmigrationOS (2025–2027):**
- April 2025: ICE awards $30M sole-source (no-bid) contract. Justification: "urgent and compelling need"; Palantir is "only source" capable of delivery in required time.
- Three functions: (1) targeting/enforcement prioritization — who gets removed first; (2) self-deportation tracking — "near real-time visibility" on voluntary departures; (3) logistics/lifecycle management — identification to removal as a single workflow.
- Data sources: IRS, Social Security, passport databases, license plates, federal systems.
- Prototype delivered September 2025. Contract runs through September 2027.
- December 2025: Palantir also wins USCIS contract — now operating on both enforcement (ICE) and benefits (USCIS) sides of the immigration system.
- February 2026: DHS signs $1B contract. Palantir is now the technical backbone of US immigration enforcement.

**Total Palantir federal contracts since Trump inauguration (Jan 2025):** $1.3B+ across 14+ agencies.

**Civil liberties assessment (ACLU, Amnesty, NYU Stern):** No public information on system accuracy. No independent oversight or Congressional debate before deployment. Errors can lead to detention, loss of status, wrongful deportation. System could be expanded to target any American. CNN: ImmigrationOS allows agents to "approve raids, book arrests, generate legal documents, and route individuals to deportation flights or detention — all from a single interface."

## IV. War Programs

**Maven Smart System — the targeting backbone:**
- Project Maven created 2017 using Google technology; Google withdrew 2018 after 4,000+ employee protests. Palantir took primary role.
- MSS fuses imagery, video, drone feeds, satellite data, sensor arrays. Machine learning for automatic object detection, classification, tracking. Reduces targeting latency from 12 hours → under 1 minute (2024+). "20–50 soldiers can analyze data that previously required hundreds or thousands."
- May 2024: $480M 5-year IDIQ. September 2024: $99.8M Army Research Lab contract. May 2025: ceiling raised $795M → $1.275B total through 2029. 20,000+ users across 35 military entities. Adopted by NATO March 2025.
- June 2025: Detachment 201 — Palantir CTO Shyam Sankar commissioned as Army Reserve lieutenant colonel alongside three other Silicon Valley executives.

**Ukraine:**
- Palantir providing battlefield intelligence since 2022 (initially reported as free).
- Karp: Palantir is "responsible for most of the targeting in Ukraine."
- Detection to target prosecution: under 2–3 minutes.
- Ukraine MoD working to formalize Palantir software use in military legal framework.
- Analysts: Palantir and Starlink turned Ukraine into "an AI war lab."

**Gaza / Israel (allegation status):**
- UN Special Rapporteur: "reasonable grounds" for Palantir complicity in Gaza operations.
- Palantir has not confirmed a direct IDF contract. Company policy: does not publicly disclose all client relationships.
- European institutional investors have begun divesting on this basis.
- Confidence: LOW on direct contract confirmation; HIGH on the UN finding itself.
