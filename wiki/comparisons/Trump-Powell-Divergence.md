## Interpretation

**EconScore (1.0 gap — lowest):** Both lean economically conservative. Trump marginally more so (5.0 vs 4.0). This is the one axis where they are closest — and the least useful for signal generation.

**AuthScore (7.5 gap):** Powell scores 0.0 — genuinely apolitical, institutional, neutral. Trump scores 7.5 — strong authoritarian-populist tendency. This gap is the primary tension in the relationship: Trump wants Fed compliance, Powell resists political pressure. Every Trump Fed comment is a signal on this axis.

**GeoScore (5.0 gap):** Trump −4.0 = isolationist/America-first. Powell +1.0 = mild internationalist, aligned with global central bank coordination (BIS, G7 Finance). This divergence feeds the DSI: Trump's isolationism accelerates de-dollarization pressure that Powell's institutional framework is designed to resist.

**EnvScore (9.5 gap — extreme):** Largest divergence in the dataset. Trump 8.5 = strongly anti-environment/deregulatory. Powell −1.0 = slightly interventionist (Fed climate risk in financial stability framework). Operationally less relevant for EUR/USD but significant for energy sector and ESG capital flows.

**CulturalScore / GenderScore / ReligionScore:** Powell 0.0 across all three — the Fed chair is deliberately culturally neutral. Trump is at the extreme end of all three (8.5 / 7.0 / 6.5). These axes are relevant for political risk premium but not directly for monetary policy divergence.

## DSI Signal Interpretation

DSI signal of **4.25** (scale 0–10, 10 = maximum divergence) means:

The Trump-Powell relationship is in **high tension territory**. The EconScore gap is small (1.0) — they broadly agree on economic conservatism. But the AuthScore gap (7.5) means Trump expects Fed deference to executive policy, and Powell is constitutionally opposed to that.

For the DSI: Trump's isolationism (GeoScore −4.0) creates structural dollar erosion pressure. Powell's institutional mandate pushes back. The net effect: dollar policy becomes unpredictable, which is itself a DSI input.

## Contrast: Powell vs Lagarde
