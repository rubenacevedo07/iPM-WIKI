# Fed Rate Policy → Markets

*Based on FOMC March 18, 2026 meeting.*

## Direct Channels
- **US rates:** Short end of curve moved higher post-meeting. "Higher for longer" reduces probability of rapid cuts. Yield curve flattening/inversion persists.
- **USD:** Appreciated vs major crosses on hawkish reading. Fed staying restrictive while ECB pauses = structural USD bid vs EUR.
- **US equities:** Mixed. Growth/tech under pressure from rate sensitivity. Banks/financials benefit from higher-for-longer.

## Second-Order Channels
- **EM capital flows:** Higher US rates = tighter global liquidity = outflows from emerging markets, especially high-external-debt countries
- **Tariff-inflation interaction:** Powell watching whether tariff price shocks become persistent. If they do, rate cuts delayed further — negative for all risk assets.
- **Fed independence risk:** Trump pressure on Powell (AuthScore gap 7.5) = political premium on USD volatility. Market prices Fed credibility risk.

## Affected Countries
- All EM dollar borrowers (higher debt service)
- [[../countries/CHINA]] — capital flow pressure
- Eurozone — EUR/USD widening

## Affected Commodities
- Gold: tends to weaken when real rates rise and USD strengthens
- Oil: mixed — USD strength = headwind, but Middle East risk = support

## Asset-Class Impact
- **Rates:** Short end higher, curve flat/inverted. 2Y UST = key signal.
- **FX:** USD bid vs EUR, EM currencies under pressure
- **Equities:** Growth/tech underperform, value/financials outperform
- **Credit:** Investment grade stable, HY spreads widen on growth fears

## Time Profile
- Short-term: USD strong, rates elevated, equities mixed
- Medium-term: If inflation data surprises lower → cuts begin → USD weakens, growth revives
- Structural: Fed credibility vs Trump pressure = ongoing volatility premium

## Key Uncertainties
- When does first cut actually happen? Market pricing vs dot plot divergence
- Do tariff price shocks become persistent?
- Does labor market weaken enough to force Fed's hand?
